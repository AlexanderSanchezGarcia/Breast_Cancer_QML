This report evaluates the viability of variational quantum transformations as an encoding mechanism for radiomic features extracted from real mammograms. On the 3,568 lesions of CBIS-DDSM, with masses and calcifications treated as independent subproblems, 67 radiomic features are extracted with PyRadiomics, 12 are selected and scaled to $[0,\pi]$. The same vector feeds five conditions: no transformation, PCA, RBF Kernel PCA, and two quantum feature maps (ZZ, and Pauli with Y and ZZ terms) followed by a fixed-parameter ansatz, simulated with Qiskit Aer. Separability is measured with kernel-target alignment, regularized geometric difference, effective dimension, the Davies-Bouldin index and the Fisher ratio; classification, with the same multilayer perceptron and patient-grouped cross-validation.

With the diagnostic labels, the quantum representations show no greater separability in any metric. The quantum kernel is concentrated (off-diagonal median close to 0.003, against 0.61 for the RBF kernel) and the geometric difference (1.40 to 1.70) stays well below the $\sqrt{N}\approx 14$ threshold. The perceptron loses between 0.07 and 0.14 AUC-ROC on the quantum embeddings, while an SVM on the quantum kernel matches the classical one, which places the loss in the expectation-value readout rather than in the encoding. The absence of advantage depends on the problem, not on the protocol: with labels engineered following the procedure of Huang et al., which aligns the classes with the geometry of the quantum kernel, the same protocol does detect the advantage, and the projected quantum kernel reaches an AUC of 0.998 on masses and 0.994 on calcifications, against 0.800 and 0.824 for the best classical classifier. With 1,024 shots, between 16 and 74\,\% of the kernel entries would read as zero.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum machine learning, quantum kernels, class separability, radiomics, CBIS-DDSM, breast cancer.
